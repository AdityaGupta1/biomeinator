# Physically Based Sky

References:
- Hillaire, "A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique" (EGSR 2020).
  Full paper is in this repo: `plans/egsr2020.pdf`, with a plain-text extraction at
  `plans/egsr2020.txt` — read the txt for equations, LUT parameterizations, and Table 1/2
  details before implementing either stage. Equation and section numbers below refer to it.
- Maxime Heckel, "On rendering the sky, sunsets and planets" (single-scattering walkthrough
  of the same LUT scheme): https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-rendering-the-sky-sunsets-and-planets/

Goal: replace the hand-tuned sky gradient in `src/shaders/light/dome_light.hlsli` with a
physically based atmosphere (Rayleigh + Mie + ozone) that responds to sun elevation, giving
correct daytime blue, sunset reddening, and twilight. Designed so a moon can be added later as
a second directional light in the same LUT generation loops.

## Atmosphere model (both stages)

Earth setup from the paper (Table 1), all coefficients per-meter ×10⁻⁶:
- Rayleigh: scattering (5.802, 13.558, 33.1), no absorption, density exp(-h/8km)
- Mie: scattering 3.996 (colorless), extinction 4.44, absorption = extinction − scattering
  ≈ 0.444, density exp(-h/1.2km), Cornette-Shanks (or Henyey-Greenstein) phase with g = 0.8.
  Note: the paper's Table 1 prints "absorption 4.40", but that value is the extinction
  (Bruneton's scattering/0.9); Hillaire's reference implementation
  (github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere) uses absorption ≈ 0.444. Absorption 4.40 would
  give a single-scatter albedo of 0.48 — soot, not haze.
- Ozone: absorption only (0.650, 1.881, 0.085), tent distribution of width 30km centered at 25km
- Ground sphere radius 6360km, atmosphere top 6460km, ground albedo 0.3
- World-to-atmosphere mapping: 1 block = 1 meter, so altitude is
  `h = max(cameraY_meters, 1m)` above the 6360km ground sphere (clamp keeps the camera off
  the exact sphere surface, where the horizon parameterization degenerates)

Shared constants and density/phase helpers go in a new `src/shaders/sky/atmosphere.hlsli` so
LUT generation shaders and `dome_light.hlsli` agree exactly.

## Stage 1 — single scattering (article version)

Two LUTs, generated by compute passes (follow the `water_displacer` pattern for a small
self-contained pass owning its textures):

**Transmittance LUT** (256×64, ~40 raymarch steps per texel):
- Stores per-wavelength transmittance from a point at altitude h along zenith angle μ to the
  atmosphere top. Bruneton-Neyret parameterization (non-linear near horizon).
- Depends only on atmosphere constants — generate once at startup, not per frame.

**Sky-view LUT** (200×100, ~30 raymarch steps per texel):
- Lat/long map of sky luminance around the camera, horizon centered, quadratic non-linear
  latitude mapping to concentrate texels at the horizon (paper §5.3).
- Raymarch accumulates single scattering per Eq. 1–4; the sun-shadowing transmittance
  `T(x, sun)` is a transmittance LUT fetch instead of a nested march.
- Regenerated every frame (sun moves with `animTime`; also tracks camera altitude).
- Longitude parameterized relative to sun azimuth so the sun-relative sky is stable.

**Integration into `dome_light.hlsli`:**
- `getSkyGradientColor` → sky-view LUT fetch from view direction. Delete zenith/horizon/ground
  constants. Below-horizon directions come from the LUT's virtual-planet ground.
- Sun disk stays separate (LUT too coarse for the disk, per paper §5.3): keep `isInSun` /
  spherical-cap NEE unchanged, but sun radiance becomes solar illuminance × transmittance LUT
  fetch at (camera altitude, sun zenith angle) — sunset reddening/dimming for free.
  `sunCosTheta = 0.9999` (~0.8° radius vs. the real 0.27°) is intentional — the sun is
  deliberately a bit larger than life; don't "correct" it.
- Sun below the horizon: the transmittance parameterization only covers rays that don't hit
  the ground sphere, and `isInSun` alone would show the disk through the horizon at night.
  Explicitly zero the sun radiance when the ray toward the sun intersects the virtual planet.
- Keep the `voxelMode` gate as-is.
- Calibrate solar illuminance against the current `sunColor` intensity (16000) so overall
  exposure and tonemapping don't shift drastically. Calibration must account for the disk's
  solid angle (larger-than-real disk at fixed radiance = more NEE energy), so match total sun
  power, not per-ray radiance.

**Consequences to handle:**
- Sky now goes black when the sun is below the horizon (current gradient doesn't). Until the
  moon exists, add a small constant ambient floor at the lookup in `dome_light.hlsli` (not
  baked into the LUT) so nights aren't pitch black; keeping it out of the LUT makes it trivial
  to delete when the moon lands.
- LUT sampling: bilinear, clamp; sky-view latitude mapping must match between generation and
  lookup (single shared uv↔direction helper in `atmosphere.hlsli`).
- Per-frame ordering: sky-view LUT dispatch + UAV barrier must run before the path trace pass
  each frame; the one-time transmittance (and later multi-scattering) generation must complete
  before the first sky-view dispatch.

**Not doing (from the article/paper):**
- Aerial perspective LUT — voxel-world distances are negligible vs. the 8km scale height, and
  a fog system (planned separately, after the sky) will own camera-to-surface scattering.
- Volumetric shadows, space views, log depth — camera is always near the ground.
- ACES tonemap — renderer already has its own tonemapping in collect.

## Stage 2 — multiple scattering LUT (paper §5.5)

Single scattering alone leaves twilight and the zenith too dark. The paper's O(1)
approximation replaces iterative per-order LUT updates:

**Multi-scattering LUT** (32×32, paper's resolution):
- Parameterized by (sun zenith angle, altitude) — Ψms is valid for any view point and light
  direction, so it's view-independent.
- Per texel, integrate over 64 uniform sphere directions (paper uses 64):
  - `L_2ndorder`: second-order in-scattered luminance toward the point (Eq. 5–6), using the
    isotropic phase function and unit illuminance, including the ground diffuse bounce
    (albedo 0.3).
  - `f_ms`: unitless energy transfer factor from the neighborhood (Eq. 7–8).
- Infinite-order sum via geometric series: `F_ms = 1 / (1 - f_ms)` (Eq. 9),
  `Ψ_ms = L_2ndorder · F_ms` (Eq. 10).
- Key approximations (paper §5.5.1): orders ≥ 2 use the isotropic phase function, uniform
  illuminance in the neighborhood, visibility ignored.

**Integration:**
- Sky-view LUT raymarch adds `Ψ_ms · E_sun` per sample alongside the phase-weighted single
  scattering term (Eq. 11). No change to `dome_light.hlsli` — it flows through the sky-view
  LUT.
- Depends only on atmosphere constants (not sun direction — that's a LUT axis), so like the
  transmittance LUT it's generated once at startup. Regenerating per frame is also nearly
  free (paper: 0.07ms on a 1080) if atmosphere parameters ever become dynamic.
- Generate order: transmittance → multi-scattering → sky-view (each reads the previous).
- When the moon is added, its illuminance uses the same LUT — Ψms multiplies any directional
  light's illuminance.
